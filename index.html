Economics of National Security Association

Rigorous economics for the questions that shape national security.

ENSA is an independent professional association connecting economists, defense researchers, and policymakers who study defense resource management, conflict, and the economics of national security — and gives that research a shared home.

Featured Research

Deterrence

Deterrence Through Response Curves: An Empirical Analysis of the Gaza-Israel Conflict

Eli Berman, Prabin B. Khadka, Danny Klinenberg & Esteban F. Klor · NBER WP 33273 (December 2024)

Models mutual deterrence as interacting response curves in a repeated game, estimated on incident-level data from the Israel-Gaza conflict, 2007–2017 — and shows Israeli missile defense shifts Gaza’s response curve toward reduced aggression, as theory predicts.

Public Finance

The Price of War

Jonathan Federle, André Meier, Gernot J. Müller, Willi Mutschler & Moritz Schularick · American Economic Review, Vol. 116, No. 3, pp. 791–827

Draws on 150 years of data across 60 countries to estimate war’s macroeconomic toll: a war of average intensity cuts output by close to 10% and pushes consumer prices up roughly 20%, with damage spilling over to trading partners and neighbors.

Read the paper →
Terrorism

Religion, Terrorism and Public Goods: Testing the Club Model

Eli Berman & David D. Laitin · Journal of Public Economics, Vol. 92, No. 10–11, pp. 1942–1967; NBER WP 13725

Models radical religious rebel groups — Hamas, Hezbollah, the Taliban — as efficient providers of local public goods that use costly sacrifice to screen out potential defectors before risky attacks.

Read the paper →
Economic Theory of War

Rationalist Explanations for War

James D. Fearon · International Organization, Vol. 49, No. 3, pp. 379–414

The foundational bargaining model of war: since fighting is costly, rational states should always be able to find a negotiated settlement both prefer to war — so war itself is the puzzle to explain.

Our Origins

“National security was the single largest component of GDP, sparsely studied by economists.” — Martin Feldstein, ENSA Founding President

ENSA grew out of a seminar on the economics of national security that Martin Feldstein ran for years at Harvard. Recognizing how little of the field was organized into a shared body of work, he brought together researchers from three Federally Funded Research and Development Centers — IDA, CNA, and RAND — along with academic and government partners, including founding member Eli Berman (UC San Diego), to formalize the association. Berman now serves as ENSA’s president.

ENSA is a professional organization and is not affiliated with any government organization.
