About ENSA

A shared home for national security economics.

Why the association exists, how it started, and who has led it.

Our Mission

Advance the field, and put it to use.

ENSA’s founding purpose was simple: have relevant information — testimony, studies, data sets, and more — on the economics of national security collected in one place, and give researchers a way of communicating with each other. That mission has since grown to include enhancing national security itself, by providing evidence and expertise for the development of public policy.

The association provides a forum for new ideas and empirical results in economic research applied to national security, defense resource management, and the public policy questions that surround them. ENSA is a professional organization and is not affiliated with any government organization.

“Have relevant information — testimony, studies, data sets, etc. on the economics of national security — collected in one place, and provide a way of communicating to others interested in the topic.” — ENSA’s founding purpose

Who it’s for. Academic economists, researchers at military academies, and professionals at defense-related think tanks and FFRDCs — anyone bringing rigorous economic analysis to bear on national security questions.
History

From a Harvard seminar to a national association.

  • The Seminar

    Martin Feldstein runs a long-standing seminar on the economics of national security at Harvard, drawing attention to how large a share of the federal budget — and how understudied a field — national security economics really was.

  • Founding

    Feldstein establishes ENSA as a national organization to formalize that community, with founding representation from three Federally Funded Research and Development Centers — IDA, CNA, and RAND — alongside academic and government-adjacent researchers.

  • Building the Field

    ENSA becomes a recurring presence at the AEA/ASSA Annual Meeting and the Western Economic Association International conference, while overlapping with NBER’s Economics of National Security research program and summer institute.

  • Today

    The association continues as a member-run professional community, with a board and officers elected periodically to guide its data, conference, and membership activities.

Board elections. ENSA holds periodic elections for its corporate board and officers as members’ terms conclude. Current board rosters and open seats are announced to the membership ahead of each election cycle — contact us for the latest.
