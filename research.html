Research

A starter reading list.

A sampling of recent and foundational scholarship on the economics of national security, organized around four recurring themes: procurement, deterrence, geoeconomics, and labor. Papers are drawn from the top-five economics journals (AER, Econometrica, JPE, QJE, ReStud) and NBER working papers. Click a theme to see its papers.

📄
Journal of Political Economy · 1987

Auctioning Incentive Contracts

R. Preston McAfee & John McMillan · Journal of Political Economy, Vol. 95, No. 5, pp. 848–872

A foundational model of how a buyer — the canonical example is a defense procurement agency — should auction a contract when bidders differ in cost efficiency and the winning contract mixes fixed-price and cost-reimbursement elements.

Read the paper →
📄
American Economic Review · 2026

Competition under Incomplete Contracts and the Design of Procurement Policies

Rodrigo Carril, Andrés Gonzalez-Lira & Michael S. Walker · American Economic Review, Vol. 116, No. 2, pp. 535–581

Studies U.S. defense procurement and finds that inviting more bidders lowers the winning price but worsens post-award performance — more cost overruns and delays. Estimates an auction model with endogenous entry and execution risk, and shows that simple tweaks to how the government publicizes contract opportunities could generate meaningful savings.

Read the paper →
📄
NBER Working Paper 25160

The Impact of Industry Consolidation on Government Procurement: Evidence from Department of Defense Contracting

Rodrigo Carril & Mark Duggan · NBER WP 25160 (2018); published in Journal of Public Economics, 2020

Using contract-level DoD data, finds that the wave of defense-industry mergers in the 1990s reduced competitive bidding and pushed contracts toward cost-plus arrangements — though the government’s buyer power limited the effect on total acquisition costs.

Read the paper →
📄
NBER Working Paper 24201

Bureaucratic Competence and Procurement Outcomes

Francesco Decarolis, Leonardo M. Giuffrida, Elisabetta Iossa, Vincenzo Mollisi & Giancarlo Spagnolo · NBER WP 24201 (2018); published in Journal of Law, Economics, & Organization, 2021

Links U.S. federal procurement contract outcomes to the competence of the purchasing bureau, finding that more capable buying offices see significantly fewer delays, cost overruns, and renegotiations.

Read the paper →
📄
NBER Working Paper 24158

The Arsenal of Democracy: Production and Politics During WWII

Paul W. Rhode, James M. Snyder Jr. & Koleman Strumpf · NBER WP 24158 (2017)

Examines the geographic allocation of over $3 trillion (current dollars) in WWII military supply contracts, finding that industrial capacity — not electoral politics — dominated where contracts landed.

Read the paper →
📄
Quarterly Journal of Economics · 2010

Conflict and Deterrence under Strategic Risk

Sylvain Chassang & Gerard Padró i Miquel · Quarterly Journal of Economics, Vol. 125, No. 4, pp. 1821–1858; NBER WP 13964

Shows that weapons unambiguously deter conflict under complete information, but under strategic risk — when parties can’t be sure how the other reads the situation — arming up can have a non-monotonic, sometimes destabilizing effect on peace.

Read the paper →
📄
Journal of Political Economy · 2008

Strategic Ambiguity and Arms Proliferation

Sandeep Baliga & Tomas Sjöström · Journal of Political Economy, Vol. 116, No. 6, pp. 1023–1057

Models a small power that can stay deliberately ambiguous about whether it holds weapons of mass destruction, and shows that ambiguity itself — not just the weapons — substitutes for costly proliferation and can benefit both sides.

Read the paper →
📄
Journal of Political Economy · 2020

The Strategy and Technology of Conflict

Sandeep Baliga & Tomas Sjöström · Journal of Political Economy, Vol. 128, No. 8, pp. 3186–3219

Finds a non-monotonic relationship between the cost of conflict and its likelihood, and shows that whether military technology favors offense or defense determines if strategic investments — like defense systems — stabilize or destabilize a rivalry.

Read the paper →
📄
NBER Working Paper 33273

Deterrence Through Response Curves: An Empirical Analysis of the Gaza-Israel Conflict

Eli Berman, Prabin B. Khadka, Danny Klinenberg & Esteban F. Klor · NBER WP 33273 (December 2024)

Models mutual deterrence as interacting response curves in a repeated game, estimated on incident-level data from the Israel-Gaza conflict, 2007–2017. Finds stable, likely Markovian response patterns consistent with incomplete deterrence, and shows Israeli missile defense shifts Gaza’s response curve toward reduced aggression — as theory predicts. Co-authored by ENSA president Eli Berman.

Read the paper →
📚
Book · Oxford University Press · 2024

The Elements of Deterrence: Strategy, Technology, and Complexity in Global Politics

Erik Gartzke & Jon R. Lindsay · Oxford University Press, 2024

Argues that Cold War deterrence theory, built for a bipolar nuclear standoff, doesn’t travel cleanly to a multipolar world of cyber, space, and conventional threats — and reworks the logic of deterrence for that more complex strategic environment.

Read about the book →
📚
Book · University of California Press · 1945

National Power and the Structure of Foreign Trade

Albert O. Hirschman · University of California Press, 1945

The founding text of geoeconomics: Hirschman showed that trade between unequal partners creates dependence a stronger state can exploit for political leverage, and proposed a measure of how concentrated a country’s trade was among a few partners — the concentration measure that, independently rediscovered by Orris Herfindahl, became the Hirschman-Herfindahl Index now standard across economics.

Read about the book →
📄
NBER Working Paper 31852

A Framework for Geoeconomics

Christopher Clayton, Matteo Maggiori & Jesse Schreger · NBER WP 31852 (2023, rev. 2024)

A formal model of geoeconomic power as the ability of a dominant economy to coordinate threats across otherwise unrelated economic relationships — identifying which sectors matter most for enforcing demands on foreign entities.

Read the paper →
📄
NBER Working Paper 34020

Geoeconomic Pressure

Christopher Clayton, Antonio Coppola, Matteo Maggiori & Jesse Schreger · NBER WP 34020 (2025)

Uses large language models to catalog real-world instances of sanctions, tariffs, export controls, and boycotts at scale, finding that firms hit with tariffs mostly adjust prices, while firms hit with export controls invest more heavily in R&D.

Read the paper →
📄
NBER Working Paper 33681

Putting Economics Back Into Geoeconomics

Christopher Clayton, Matteo Maggiori & Jesse Schreger · NBER WP 33681 (2025)

A survey connecting the political-science concept of geoeconomics to standard economic tools, mapping out how different threat mechanisms can be modeled and where the open questions for economists are.

Read the paper →
📄
NBER Working Paper 31024

International Sanctions and Dollar Dominance

Javier Bianchi & César Sosa-Padilla · NBER WP 31024 (2023, rev. 2025)

Models how the anticipation of financial sanctions erodes the convenience yield on dollar assets, with welfare costs for both the sanctioning and sanctioned economies.

Read the paper →
📄
NBER Working Paper 29989

On Wars, Sanctions and Sovereign Default

Javier Bianchi & César Sosa-Padilla · NBER WP 29989 (2022, rev. 2023)

A strategic model of reserve-freezing sanctions calibrated to Russia’s 2022 default, estimating that for every dollar of economic damage inflicted on the sanctioned country, the sanctioning country gives up about fifty cents of its own consumption.

Read the paper →
📄
American Economic Review · 1990

Lifetime Earnings and the Vietnam Era Draft Lottery: Evidence from Social Security Administrative Records

Joshua D. Angrist · American Economic Review, Vol. 80, No. 3, pp. 313–336

Uses the randomized Vietnam-era draft lottery as an instrument for veteran status, finding that white veterans earned roughly 15% less than comparable nonveterans well into the 1980s — a founding application of instrumental variables to military labor economics.

Read the paper →
📄
Econometrica · 1998

Estimating the Labor Market Impact of Voluntary Military Service Using Social Security Data on Military Applicants

Joshua D. Angrist · Econometrica, Vol. 66, No. 2, pp. 249–288; NBER WP 5192

Compares applicants who enlisted to those who didn’t to isolate the effect of voluntary service, finding large earnings gains while in uniform and modest post-service gains for nonwhite veterans — driven mainly by improved civilian employment prospects.

Read the paper →
📄
Journal of Political Economy · 2024

The Effects of Combat Deployments on Veterans’ Outcomes

Jesse Bruhn, Kyle Greenberg, Matthew Gudgeon, Evan K. Rose & Yotam Shem-Tov · Journal of Political Economy, Vol. 132, No. 8, pp. 2830–2879; NBER WP 30622

The all-volunteer-era counterpart to Angrist’s draft-lottery work: uses quasi-random assignment to combat deployments to show they raise injuries, combat deaths, and disability compensation, but have limited effect on suicide, financial health, incarceration, or education.

Read the paper →
📄
NBER Working Paper 7452

Going to War and Going to College: Did World War II and the G.I. Bill Increase Educational Attainment for Returning Veterans?

John Bound & Sarah E. Turner · NBER WP 7452 (1999); published in Journal of Labor Economics, 2002

Uses variation in draft exposure and cohort size across states to net out selection, finding that WWII service and the G.I. Bill produced a moderate net gain in postsecondary educational attainment for veterans.

Read the paper →
📄
NBER Working Paper 32774

World War II Service and the G.I. Bill: New Evidence on Selection and Veterans’ Outcomes from Linked Census Records

William J. Collins & Ariell Zimran · NBER WP 32774 (2024)

Links individual-level Census records across decades to separate who selected into service from what service caused, finding younger WWII veterans gained in both education and labor market outcomes relative to similar nonveterans.

Read the paper →
📄
NBER Working Paper 33382

The Impact of World War II Army Service on Income and Mobility in the 1960s by Ethnoracial Group

Sergio E. Barrera, Andreas Ferrara, Price V. Fishback & Misty L. Heggeness · NBER WP 33382 (January 2025)

Links 1940 Census, enlistment, and 1969 tax records to study how WWII Army service affected income and migration across racial and ethnic groups, finding that once selection is properly accounted for, the earnings and mobility effects of service were uneven — and often negative — across groups.

Read the paper →
